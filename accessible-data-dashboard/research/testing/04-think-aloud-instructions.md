# Think-aloud instructions (read aloud)

> While you work, please say out loud what you're looking at, what you're trying to do and what you're thinking, even small things like "I'm not sure what this means" or "I expected that to do something else". Imagine you're explaining it to a colleague who can't see your screen.
>
> If you go quiet I might ask "What are you thinking?" That's just a reminder, not a sign that you're doing anything wrong.
>
> **If you use a screen reader:** your screen reader will be talking too, so it's fine to pause it whenever you want to comment, or to comment after it finishes. Do whatever is comfortable. You don't have to narrate every keystroke.

## Practice task (not scored)
Open `BASE` and:
> "Without changing anything, tell me what this page is about and what you'd look at first."

Encourage them once if they go quiet. Then start T1.

## Moderator notes
- Talking can slow people down, so time-on-task is approximate. Compare it across tasks within one participant, not between AT and non-AT participants without context.
- Concurrent think-aloud can be tiring for screen reader users, because the speech output and their own voice compete. Offer **retrospective** think-aloud instead: they complete the task quietly, then talk you through it. Note which method was used for each task.
