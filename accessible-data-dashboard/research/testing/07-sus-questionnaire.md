# System Usability Scale (accessible version)

The SUS (Brooke, 1996) is used here with its standard 10 items. "System" is replaced by "dashboard", a substitution commonly used in practice. Two accessible ways to run it:

1. **Read aloud** (default for screen reader users and anyone who prefers it). Read each statement and ask for a number from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Repeat the scale at least every three statements.
2. **Form.** Send the table below as plain text, or build a form with **one fieldset per statement**: the `legend` is the statement, and there are five labelled radio buttons ("1, strongly disagree" … "5, strongly agree"). Don't use a grid or matrix layout; many form tools render those inaccessibly. Test the form with a screen reader before sending it.

Participants may skip any statement. Record skipped statements as missing, not as 3.

| # | Statement | 1 Strongly disagree | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 Strongly agree |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I think that I would like to use this dashboard frequently. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 2 | I found the dashboard unnecessarily complex. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 3 | I thought the dashboard was easy to use. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 4 | I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this dashboard. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 5 | I found the various functions in this dashboard were well integrated. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 6 | I thought there was too much inconsistency in this dashboard. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 7 | I would imagine that most people would learn to use this dashboard very quickly. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 8 | I found the dashboard very cumbersome to use. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 9 | I felt very confident using the dashboard. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 10 | I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this dashboard. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

## Scoring
- Odd items (1, 3, 5, 7, 9): score = answer − 1.
- Even items (2, 4, 6, 8, 10): score = 5 − answer.
- SUS = sum × 2.5 (0–100).
- If one item is missing, you can prorate: (sum ÷ answered items) × 10 × 2.5. Report that you did. If more than one is missing, don't calculate a score.

## Reporting with n = 5
Report each participant's score, the mean and the range. Treat them as **indicative only**: five participants are not enough for benchmark comparisons or for AT vs non-AT comparisons. The qualitative findings carry the weight.

*Reference: Brooke, J. (1996). SUS: A "quick and dirty" usability scale. In P. W. Jordan et al. (Eds.), Usability Evaluation in Industry. Taylor & Francis.*
